## Comparison of the three metaheuristics (9 November 2026)

**Question.** Which metaheuristic gives the lowest gap to the best known solution (BKS) at equal
time, and is the difference significant?

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Algorithms | Tabu Search (Pablo), Simulated Annealing (Dag), GRASP-ILS (Farida) |
| Instances | 56 Solomon CVRPTW instances, n = 100; distances truncated to one decimal (BKS convention) |
| Tuning | C101, C201, R101, R201, RC101, RC201, seeds 101 and 102 (108 to 144 runs per algorithm) |
| Test | the other 50 instances, seeds 1 to 10, 500 runs per algorithm |
| Budget | 20 s per run, construction included |
| Machine | one Apple M2, 7 runs in parallel, jobs of the three algorithms interleaved |
| Feasibility | GRASP-ILS and SA checked by the shared evaluator, Tabu Search by its cost function (capacity, windows, return) |
| Commands | `python experiments/solomon_study.py tune`, then `run`, then `python experiments/solomon_report.py` |

**Chosen parameters** (lowest mean gap on the tuning set)

| Algorithm | Parameters | Tuning gap (%) | Settings tried |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GRASP-ILS | alpha 0.3, first improvement, 90 kicks | 1.17 | 12 |
| SA | initial temperature factor 0.005, final temperature 0.005 | 0.56 | 12 |
| Tabu Search | tenure 20, 200 candidates | 2.11 | 9 |

*Interpretation:* the SA settings all lie within 0.3 points of each other, so its choice matters
little; for Tabu Search and GRASP the setting matters (worst settings at 10 % and 3 %).

### Overall (50 test instances, 10 seeds)

| Algorithm | Mean gap (%) | Std | Median | Worst | Runs at BKS | Runs within 7 % | Mean routes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **GRASP-ILS** | **1.16** | 1.41 | 0.68 | 9.82 | 162 | 99.2 % | 8.0 |
| SA | 1.70 | 1.43 | 1.58 | 6.51 | 106 | 100 % | 8.9 |
| Tabu Search | 3.98 | 3.15 | 3.96 | 14.03 | 110 | 83.2 % | 9.1 |

*Interpretation:* all 1500 runs are feasible; GRASP-ILS has the lowest mean and median gap, SA the
smallest worst case, and Tabu Search has 3.4 times the mean gap of GRASP-ILS. The brief caps the
grade when the gap exceeds 7 to 10 %: GRASP-ILS and SA stay within the stricter 7 % on 99 to 100 %
of their runs.

### By class (mean gap, %)

| Class | GRASP-ILS | SA | Tabu Search |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | **0.02** | 0.44 | 0.78 |
| C2 | **0.01** | 0.73 | 1.26 |
| R1 | **0.68** | 1.59 | 3.54 |
| R2 | **2.22** | 2.37 | 5.78 |
| RC1 | **1.30** | 2.39 | 5.62 |
| RC2 | 2.73 | **2.65** | 6.82 |

*Interpretation:* GRASP-ILS wins five classes out of six; SA is slightly ahead on RC2, and the
type 2 classes (few long routes) are the hardest for all three.

### Statistical tests (per-instance mean gap, 50 instances)

| Test | Statistic | p | Holm p | Better on (A / B / ties) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Friedman, three algorithms | chi2 = 57.1 | 4.0e-13 | | |
| Wilcoxon GRASP-ILS vs SA | W = 194 | 7.5e-04 | 7.5e-04 | 31 / 12 / 7 |
| Wilcoxon GRASP-ILS vs Tabu Search | W = 0 | 2.4e-08 | 7.3e-08 | 41 / 0 / 9 |
| Wilcoxon SA vs Tabu Search | W = 19 | 2.8e-08 | 7.3e-08 | 40 / 4 / 6 |

*Interpretation:* every difference is significant at 5 % after the Holm correction (Wilcoxon drops
the tied instances, so it uses 43, 41 and 44 of the 50).

**Decision.** By the rule fixed before the campaign (feasible on every run, then mean gap, then
mean time), **GRASP-ILS** is the chosen method. Caveat: SA is compiled (numba) and the other two
are pure Python, so equal time mixes the algorithm with its implementation; this favors SA, which
still comes second.

## Round 2: scale, real road networks, night (12 November 2026)

Asked at the review of 10 November. Same machine, tuned settings, shared evaluator; section 9 of
the final notebook. Commands: `python experiments/scale_study.py homberger`, `... synthetic`,
`python experiments/city_study.py run`, `... control`, `python experiments/sensitivity.py <algo>`.

**From 100 to 1 000 customers** (Homberger R1, time windows, mean gap to BKS over 3 seeds, %)

| Customers, budget | GRASP-ILS | SA | Tabu Search |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 400, 20 s | 3.67 | 5.16 | 15.37 |
| 800, 20 s | 8.40 | 14.36 | 32.32 |
| 1 000, 20 s | 9.91 | 21.75 | 37.33 |
| 1 000, 120 s | 7.81 | 10.45 | 31.74 |

*Interpretation:* GRASP-ILS leads at every size and budget; SA gains most from time (its gap halves
from 20 to 120 s); Tabu Search's gap is mostly extra trucks (175 routes against 107 at 1 000, 20 s).

**10 000 customers** (uniform synthetic CVRP, 300 s, one run each)

| Algorithm | Result | Trucks | Above the radial lower bound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tabu Search | 502 257 | 553 (minimum) | 18.6 % |
| SA | 779 401 | 774 | 84.0 % |
| GRASP-ILS | no plan after 684 s (first iteration unfinished) | | |

*Interpretation:* only Tabu Search scales to 10 000 customers within the budget, through its
neighbor lists. A sweep at 1 000, 2 000 and 5 000 customers (60 s, `scale_study.py sweep`) places
the limit of GRASP-ILS: one construction plus its first descent takes about 2 s at 1 000,
10 s at 2 000 and 133 s at 5 000 (budget 60 s; `scale_study.py grasp_start`);
Tabu Search goes from 0.7 to 2.1 ms per iteration between 1 000 and 10 000 customers.

**Real road networks** (999 customers, distance in km, mean of 5 seeds, 60 s)

| City | GRASP-ILS | SA | Tabu Search |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paris | 325.1 | 420.7 | 334.9 |
| São Paulo | 359.2 | 459.8 | 382.9 |

*Interpretation:* without windows GRASP-ILS leads in both countries (3.3 % and 2.7 % above the best
run, Tabu Search 6.4 % and 9.5 %, SA about 33 %); the ranking is the same in Paris and São Paulo.
*Correction:* a first version reported Tabu Search ahead; the 2-opt of GRASP-ILS assumed a
symmetric matrix and cycled on these one-way street matrices. Fixed (with a test) and rerun.

**Real road networks with the time windows of the model** (999 customers, 120 min windows between
08:00 and 18:00, 5 min of service, 60 s, 5 seeds; `city_study.py windows` and `city_pilot.py`)

| Objective | GRASP-ILS | SA | Tabu Search |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free-flow travel time, % above the best run (Paris, São Paulo) | 2.9, 2.8 | 20.5, 23.2 | 7.2, 9.8 |
| Duration with hourly traffic, 999 customers | 5.8, 7.9 | no traffic mode | 0.5, 0.8 |
| Duration with hourly traffic, 300 customers (pilot size) | 3.1, 5.8 | no traffic mode | 0.9, 0.9 |

*Interpretation:* with a static objective GRASP-ILS wins, with or without windows; with hourly
traffic Tabu Search wins, because GRASP-ILS recomputes the exact duration of every candidate route
(construction and descent 30.8 s against 1.9 s at 999 customers; `city_pilot.py timing`). Routes last about 6 h and cross several traffic periods.
Recommendation for the ADEME pilot (windows and traffic): Tabu Search; for a static CVRPTW:
GRASP-ILS.

**Night** (duration with hourly traffic, Tabu Search): leaving at 22:00 instead of 08:00 cuts the
total duration from 1 831 to 1 014 min in Paris (44.6 %) and from 1 511 to 850 min in São Paulo
(43.7 %). This is the traffic factor of 8:00 (1 − 1/1.80 = 44.4 %): routes are short, only 1 of 57
changes hour, so the experiment measures the size of the traffic effect, not a change of plan.

## Exact approach (Pablo)

`python experiments/exact_study.py`, HiGHS on one thread, 60 s per solve; details in section 4 of
the final notebook.

| Model | Largest n proven optimal in 60 s | Remark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CVRP, MTZ | 8 to 12 | weak LP relaxation (root gap 50 %) |
| CVRP, flow and capacity cuts | 16 (largest tested), at most 4.4 s | root gap 23 % and 0.6 % |
| CVRPTW, distance | 100 on C101 and R101 (3 s), 25 on RC101 | optima equal the BKS 827.3 and 1637.7 |
| Model of notebook 01, with traffic | 7 to 8 | LP relaxation gives no bound |

*Interpretation:* without traffic the ILP is the reference on small or easy cases and checks the
BKS convention; with traffic it only validates the objective on small cases, so the metaheuristic
is needed beyond 8 customers.
